Which describes you?

For students

Middle school, high school, and college students who want to make a consistent and meaningful difference.

What we ask

  • A weekly phone call, video call, letter, or other approved check-in
  • In-person time about once a month when feasible and permitted
  • One kindness or community activity each month
  • A simple contact log, so we can see that matches are staying consistent

After you apply, we confirm consent and your emergency contact, finish required screening and training, and send you and your match a written introduction. A program leader checks in after about 1 week, 1 month, and 2 months.

Volunteer boundaries

  • Students are companions, not caregivers, nurses, or emergency responders
  • No handling medication, banking, credit cards, passwords, legal documents, or money
  • No lifting, bathing, toileting, feeding help, wound care, or other personal care
  • No taking or asking for significant gifts, loans, or money
  • No transporting a senior unless our parent nonprofit has approved it
  • Don’t enter a private home alone. Minors use approved common areas or another adult-present arrangement
  • No photos, recordings, or posts of a senior without written permission
  • Report any concern right away to the designated adult

About Students Supporting Seniors

A small idea: people of different ages should know each other.

Who we are

Students Supporting Seniors (SSS) is a student-led community service initiative that brings generations together through friendship, companionship, kindness, and meaningful connection.

SSS is an initiative of A Better Tomorrow Montgomery County / JDR Community Initiatives. A Better Tomorrow Montgomery County is a registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization.

Our goal is simple: create genuine relationships between students and older adults while giving young people a meaningful, structured way to serve their communities.

Led by Sophia, the initiative connects students with seniors through meaningful service, companionship, and community engagement.

Photo of Sophia

Meet Sophia

Hi! I’m Sophia, founder of Students Supporting Seniors.

For years, I’ve spent time with seniors in my community through visits to retirement homes, nursing homes, and memory care centers. I’ve delivered meals, helped with a Meals on Wheels fundraiser, and, most of all, listened to their stories and wisdom.

When I joined National Charity League in seventh grade, volunteering became more structured, but my connection to seniors started long before that.

Through speech and debate, I’ve given speeches about loneliness and learned how to turn listening into action. Hearing seniors’ experiences has helped me understand how important companionship, community support, and accessible resources can be.

I founded Students Supporting Seniors to bring more students into this work: to serve, build friendships, develop leadership skills, and advocate for better resources and stronger community connections so older adults feel less alone.

My goal is to create meaningful change by listening to seniors, helping others understand their needs, and working together to take action.

“I’ve spent years listening to their stories. Now I want to help make sure their voices are heard.”

Safety and FAQ

Safe, respectful friendships come first. Here is how we protect students and older adults, and answers to common questions.

How we keep everyone safe

Adult oversight

Every chapter has an approved adult adviser. One adult program director can pause a match, address incidents, and keep records.

Consent and check-ins

Parent or guardian consent is required for minor volunteers. Every participant has an emergency contact, and organized visits use check-in and check-out.

Clear boundaries

Students are companions. They don’t handle money or medication, give personal care, or enter a private home alone.

Speaking up

Concerns go straight to a designated adult. Either person can pause or end a match at any time, without penalty.

Visit safety

  • Students give the program director the schedule and location of organized in-person visits
  • If a senior doesn’t answer an expected call or visit, students follow our no-answer protocol. They don’t enter, search, or contact emergency services on their own unless there is an immediate, apparent emergency
  • Minors meet in approved common areas or another approved adult-present setting
  • Students don’t transport seniors unless our parent nonprofit has approved a transportation policy and insurance coverage

If there’s a concern

  • Students report suspected abuse, neglect, exploitation, threats, unsafe conditions, harassment, boundary violations, or concerning behavior to the designated adult right away
  • The adult program leader decides next steps under applicable law and our policies. Students don’t investigate
  • Incidents are documented factually and kept confidential, with limited access

Privacy

  • No photos, recordings, or social media posts of a senior without written permission
  • We track only what is useful, such as contact logs and service hours, and avoid collecting unnecessary private details
  • Stories and results are shared only with permission

What students learn in training

  • What companionship means
  • Respectful communication and active listening
  • Ageism, and treating older adults as individuals
  • Privacy and social media rules
  • Boundaries around money, medical help, transportation, gifts, physical help, and private homes
  • What to do if a senior is upset, confused, unwell, doesn’t answer, or shares a safety concern
  • How to log contact and request help or a rematch

Frequently asked questions

What is Students Supporting Seniors?

SSS connects student volunteers with older adults to build meaningful intergenerational relationships through regular conversation, companionship, kindness projects, and community activities. It reduces isolation while giving students a structured way to serve, listen, learn, and build lasting community connections.

Who can volunteer?

Middle school, high school, and college students who want to make a consistent and meaningful difference in their communities.

Who can be matched with a student?

Older adults who would welcome more companionship, conversation, and connection with younger generations. You don’t have to live alone or feel isolated. Participants live independently, with family, or in senior living communities.

How much time does it take?

Students are asked to make weekly contact by phone, video, letter, or another approved method, meet in person once a month when feasible and permitted, and join one kindness or community activity each month.

How are matches made?

We consider shared interests and conversation topics, schedules, preferred contact method and frequency, location and approved visit setting, and language and communication needs. We also consider comfort with pets, mobility and accessibility needs, and family involvement.

Do students provide care or help with errands?

No. Students are not caregivers, nurses, therapists, home-health workers, financial helpers, drivers, or emergency responders. They offer companionship and age-appropriate service.

What if the match isn’t a good fit?

Either person can ask for a change confidentially. The program director closes the match, notes the reason at a high level, and decides about a rematch. We never promise a perfect match. Graduating students give notice so a senior isn’t left without communication.

Will photos be taken?

Only with written permission from the senior, or from the parent or guardian of a minor.

Where do you serve?

SSS is being developed for the North Houston region, starting across Harris and Montgomery Counties. See the Who we support page for the list of communities.

Who runs SSS?

SSS is student-led, led by Sophia, and is an initiative of A Better Tomorrow Montgomery County / JDR Community Initiatives, with adult oversight.

How can my school or organization get involved?

Start a school chapter or become a community partner on our Chapters and partners page.

Chapters and partners

We work with schools, senior communities, churches, senior centers, and community organizations to create safe opportunities for intergenerational connection.

Start a school chapter

Bring SSS to your school with student leaders and an adult adviser.

Chapter leadership

  • Student President / Executive Lead: coordinates student leadership and chapter goals
  • Vice President / Operations: supports matching events, calendars, and chapter reporting
  • Senior Outreach Lead: coordinates approved community partner outreach
  • Student Recruitment / Training Lead: supports volunteer onboarding and training
  • Communications Lead: manages approved newsletters, stories, and social content
  • Service / Kindness Project Lead: plans monthly group projects
  • Adult Adviser / Program Director: keeps adult oversight and safety authority

Chapter standards

  • Each chapter has an approved adult adviser
  • All volunteers complete the same core training and forms
  • Chapters use central SSS branding and don’t create independent legal or financial commitments
  • A monthly report covers active volunteers, active matches, contacts and visits, service hours, activities, concerns, and upcoming plans
  • Leadership transition begins before the end of each school year
